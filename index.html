About

          
Every customer should leave better off than they arrived.

          

            I have spent a bit over three years in support for Shopify apps, mostly with merchants in the US, UK and
            EU. The job is simple to describe and hard to do well: help people get what they came for, and make sure
            they still like us afterwards.
          

          

            Most of my day is live chat and email, with the odd video call. I ask what a merchant is trying to do, work
            out whether the app can do it, and if it can't, I scope the custom work and we build it for them. I also run
            the support and QA teams, which means rosters, onboarding new people, and signing off releases. AI helps me
            with most of it, but I read everything before it goes out.
          

          

            When I'm not working I'm usually on a motorbike, somewhere new, or on a cricket pitch.

AI workflow

        
How I use AI, every day.

        

          Four things I do with AI most working days. In each case it does the first pass and I check the
          result before a customer or a report sees it.
        


        

          

            

              01
              
Vibe coding with Claude Code

              
I build custom store designs, demo stores and quick demos of new features, so a merchant can see the thing working before anyone commits to building it properly.

            

            

              02
              
Trained AI threads for replies

              
I keep Claude and ChatGPT threads loaded with our product docs. I paste in the customer's message, get a draft back, fix whatever is off, and send it. Gmail's built-in AI covers the quick ones.

            

            

              03
              
AI-assisted issue reproduction

              
I point AI at the merchant's store while I reproduce the issue. It reads the theme and the page faster than I can, which gets me to the real cause sooner.

            

            

              04
              
MCP + automated QA

              
ClickUp, Google Drive, Figma and a test store are connected over MCP, and a Playwright and Chromium setup runs most of our test cases. I go through the results, add the manual and edge-case checks, and send the report.

              02
              

                
The data was not missing. Shopify was hiding it.

                
Technical troubleshooting · Shopify internals · Liquid · Product feedback

              

            


            

              The cause was one line of debug output. A same-day fix for the merchant,
              and the permanent one handed to the product team.
            


            

              Read the full story

              

                
The problem

                

                  A gift-box brand using PushBundle's Build-Your-Own-Box sells personalised sets. Their
                  warehouse works from printed packing slips and CSV exports. The customer's choices and form
                  answers — the name, the address, the gift details — showed on the admin order page
                  but not on the packing slip or in the CSV, so the warehouse was packing blind.
                  Everyone assumed the app was not saving the data.
                


                
What I did

                
    
                  
  1. Built the same bundle on a test store and read the cart. The app was writing everything: one cart line, 11 properties.
    2. 
                  
  2. Placed the order. At checkout, Shopify's native bundle expansion replaced that one line with 7 component lines. The original line, and its properties, no longer existed as a line item.
    3. 
                  
  3. Wrote Liquid debug probes into the packing-slip template. They printed [p:0 g:1]: zero properties on the lines, one bundle group. The data lives on a line item group, and Shopify only lets packing-slip Liquid read a group's title.
    4. 
                  
  4. Confirmed it against Shopify's own documentation and developer forum, and checked the CSV route too. The native export has no properties column, and export apps read the empty component lines.
    5. 
                


                

                
The fix, in two layers

                
    
                  
  • Same day, for the merchant: a packing-slip template that carries the answers through order-level cart attributes, which bundle expansion does not touch. The answers printed on the slip again.
    • 
                  
  • Properly, in the product: a change handed to the PushBundle team. When the bundle expands, write the answers onto the first component line using Shopify's Cart Transform attributes, available since API 2024-04. That one change fixes the packing slip, the CSV exports and any warehouse integration in one go.
    • 
                


                
The result

                
    
                  
  • The fix went live on the client's store, and the client confirmed it worked.
    • 
                  
  • It closed a blind spot that affected every PushBundle merchant using native bundles.
    • 
                


                

                  The client was happy enough to ask us to build a whole product-customiser app for their
                  engraving products. I advised against it. A custom app that size would have cost them
                  far more than an existing customiser app from the Shopify App Store, so I pointed them to
                  those instead. The right answer is not always the one that bills more.
                

              

            

          


          
          

            

              03
              

                
Development got faster with AI. Testing did not. So I fixed testing.

                
Team leadership · QA · Automation · Built with AI

              

            


            

              One ticket every two days became three or more a day, for the whole QA team,
              across 100+ tickets.
            


            

              Read the full story

              

                
The problem

                

                  We wrote every test case by hand, tested by hand and wrote every report by hand. One ticket
                  took about two days, and grey areas still went untested. Once the dev team moved to AI,
                  features arrived faster than we could check them. QA became the bottleneck for the whole company.
                


                
What I did

                

                  I looked into how Claude could connect to the tools we already use. I connected it over MCP to
                  ClickUp, Playwright with Chromium, and our demo store. Then I built the Shopify QA Suite myself
                  with vibe coding. People only handle the small part that needs human judgment.
                


                
    
                  
  1. TicketReads the ClickUp ticket, the design and the requirements doc
    2. 
                  
  2. Test sheetWrites the cases into a sheet, grouped by area
    3. 
                  
  3. Real browserRuns them against the live app and the storefront
    4. 
                  
  4. ReportResults, plus an issues doc with repro steps and screenshots
    5. 
                


                
The result

                
    
                  
  • Two days per ticket became three or more tickets a day.
    • 
                  
  • Used by the whole QA team. More than 100 tickets tested so far.
    • 
                  
  • More cases per ticket. About 125 cases across 16 areas for one PushBundle pricing ticket, and 135 across 17 for a DiscountRay one.
    • 
                  
  • Real bugs caught before merchants saw them.
    •
