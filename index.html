01

Won back a merchant who had uninstalled

She left, so I rebuilt her product on a demo store and built the 30% the app could not do. Four days later she was on two yearly plans, about $370 a year.

Read the whole story

The problem

She uninstalled MultiVariants. She needed something that sounds simple but no single app did out of the box: cards sold in packs of 10, any designs mixed together, a volume price applied in the cart, and no big pricing table on the product page. She wrote to us through Google Translate and preferred email to calls.

What I did

  1. Had a teammate send a short win-back email asking why she had left. Her reply told us exactly what she needed.
  2. Looked through her store and rebuilt her actual product on my demo store.
  3. Found the app covered about 70%. I built the other 30% with Liquid and JavaScript.
  4. Sent her a short video of her own product working, not a generic demo.
  5. The mix-and-match discount suited DiscountRay better, so I demoed that too, with her real prices.
  6. Set up both apps in her store, then worked through her follow-ups one by one — including the module vanishing when the store switched to English, which was her product type being translated from French so the ruleset no longer matched.

The result

  • Uninstalled 26 September, reinstalled 30 September.
  • Two yearly plans: MultiVariants Pro, the top plan, and DiscountRay Growth. About $370 a year.
  • Five-star reviews on the Shopify App Store and Trustpilot, for both apps, unprompted.

02

The data was not missing. Shopify was hiding it.

Form answers vanished from the packing slip. Liquid debug probes printed [p:0 g:1] — the answers had moved to a line item group the slip cannot read.

Read the whole story

The problem

A gift-box brand using PushBundle’s Build-Your-Own-Box sells personalised sets. Their warehouse works from printed packing slips and CSV exports. The customer’s choices showed on the admin order page but not on the slip or in the CSV, so the warehouse was packing blind. Everyone assumed the app was not saving the data.

What I did

  1. Built the same bundle on a test store and read the cart. The app was writing everything: one cart line, 11 properties.
  2. Placed the order. At checkout, Shopify’s native bundle expansion replaced that one line with 7 component lines. The original line, and its properties, no longer existed as a line item.
  3. Wrote Liquid debug probes into the packing-slip template. They printed [p:0 g:1]: zero properties on the lines, one bundle group. Shopify only lets packing-slip Liquid read a group’s title.
  4. Confirmed it against Shopify’s own documentation and developer forum, and checked the CSV route too.

The fix, in two layers

  • Same day: a packing-slip template carrying the answers through order-level cart attributes, which bundle expansion does not touch.
  • Properly: a change handed to the PushBundle team — write the answers onto the first component line using Cart Transform attributes. That fixes the slip, the CSV exports and any warehouse integration at once.

The client then asked us to build a whole product-customiser app. I advised against it — an existing app from the App Store would cost them far less. The right answer is not always the one that bills more.

03

Development got faster with AI. Testing did not.

So I built the QA Suite. One ticket every two days became three or more a day, for the whole team, across 100+ tickets.

Read the whole story

The problem

We wrote every test case by hand, tested by hand and wrote every report by hand. One ticket took about two days, and grey areas still went untested. Once the dev team moved to AI, features arrived faster than we could check them. QA became the bottleneck for the whole company.

What I did

I connected Claude over MCP to ClickUp, Playwright with Chromium, and our demo store, then built the Shopify QA Suite. It reads the ticket, writes the cases into a sheet, runs them against the live app and the storefront, and produces the report with repro steps and screenshots. People only handle the part that needs human judgment.

The result

  • Two days per ticket became three or more tickets a day.
  • Used by the whole QA team, more than 100 tickets so far.
  • About 125 cases across 16 areas for one PushBundle pricing ticket, and 135 across 17 for a DiscountRay one.
  • Real bugs caught before merchants saw them — a bundle that reached checkout at zero, and a fixed discount read as a percentage after a currency switch.
